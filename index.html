01 ABSTRACT

What does the enrollment record reveal?

This paper presents a reproducible, descriptive analysis of a year of EduPro enrollment transactions. It examines how activity is distributed across learner age cohorts, course categories, instructional levels, and gender groups—and how those patterns compare with catalog supply.

The study combines validated workbook ingestion with three transparent analytical views: the Category Popularity Index (CPI), adjusted gender participation ratios, and aggregate review flags. Direct identifiers are removed before analysis, and small demographic cells are suppressed.

03 STUDY DESIGN

Built for traceability.

A cross-sectional descriptive analysis of the 2025 workbook snapshot.

The analytical unit is a validated transaction row, interpreted by the application as an enrollment record. Four workbook sheets—Users, Teachers, Courses, and Transactions—are checked against required schemas, normalized, and joined on identifiers. Transaction IDs are deduplicated; records with invalid dates, negative amounts, or orphan references are excluded by the loader.

For this snapshot, all 10,000 transaction rows passed validation. The valid analytical table spans January 1 through December 30, 2025. Enrollment is an activity proxy: this dataset does not record course completion, learning gains, or session-level participation.

Separate review-only screens compare paid amounts within a course using a modified Z-score (minimum eight paid records; absolute score above 3.5), flag learner activity above the 99th percentile, and compare daily counts with weekday peers when the date-series and peer-count minimums are met. These full-snapshot flags do not alter or exclude records.

04 FINDINGS

Patterns, not verdicts.

Results are limited to the supplied transaction and catalog fields.

A

Activity is concentrated among learners under 36.

The 26–35 cohort accounts for 47.99% of enrollments, followed by 18–25 at 37.32% and Under 18 at 14.69%. There are no 36+ enrollments in this extract. This is a gap in observed coverage—not evidence about demand or ability among older adults.

47.99%AGES 26–35
B

Observed catalog uptake is broadly proportional to supply.

Each of the 12 categories has five catalog courses. Category CPI values range from 0.97 to 1.10, remaining within the application’s 0.85–1.15 “balanced” range. Data Science is the upper end (916 enrollments; CPI 1.10); Programming is at the lower end (806; CPI 0.97).

0.97–1.10CPI RANGE
C

Aggregate gender shares are close; category patterns vary.

Female-coded enrollment records represent 50.78% and male-coded records 49.22%. At category level, four of twelve indices fall within the project reference band. The lowest is Marketing (0.89); these ratios describe participation distribution, not fairness, preference, or cause.

D

Free access is a substantial part of recorded activity.

6,403 records (64.03%) are zero-dollar enrollments; 3,597 (35.97%) are paid. Transaction amount describes recorded monetary value only and cannot establish course value, completion, or learning effectiveness.

05 IMPLICATIONS

From evidence to next questions.

01⌖

Investigate reach

Study why older age cohorts are absent from this snapshot using additional, ethically collected data and learner research—not assumptions from zero counts.

RESEARCH OPPORTUNITY
02◒

Monitor, don’t overreact

Track category CPI over multiple periods before reallocating catalog capacity. A single-year ratio cannot show persistent unmet demand or course quality.

PLANNING PRACTICE
03⌁

Add outcome evidence

Pair enrollment telemetry with completion, learner-reported experience, and learning measures—using consent-aware collection and privacy review.

06 LIMITATIONS & ETHICS

Read the boundaries
as carefully as the results.

A dashboard can make evidence easier to inspect. It cannot make observational data answer questions it was not designed to answer.

  • Descriptive, not causal. Associations and share ratios do not explain why learners enrolled or establish intervention effects.
  • Enrollment is not learning. There are no completion, assessment, engagement-duration, or learning-gain measures in this analysis.
  • One bounded snapshot. The 2025 workbook may not represent other periods, populations, or platforms; category mix is evenly sized here.
  • Group labels have limits. Gender values are recorded binary in this extract; the analysis cannot represent identities absent from the source data.
  • No population denominator. The workbook describes enrolled users but does not supply the eligible learner population; these shares cannot quantify population-level access or exclusion.
  • Suppression is not anonymity. k=5 display suppression reduces exposure of small cells, but does not guarantee protection against linkage or inference, especially across overlapping queries.
  • Review flags are not diagnoses. Robust deviation flags nominate aggregates for human review; they are not fraud, misconduct, or error findings.
